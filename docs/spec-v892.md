# spec-v892 — Post-paracentesis albumin

## What this gives you

The albumin owed after a large-volume paracentesis, in grams and in bottles — and the two things
about that number that are routinely got wrong.

## §1 The rule

| Volume removed | |
|---|---|
| **Above 5 L** | 6–8 g of albumin **per liter removed**, across the whole volume |
| **5 L or less** | Not routinely required for the volume alone |

## §2 The trigger is the volume, not the serum albumin

This is why the tile exists. Post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction follows the **volume
shift**, so the patient's albumin level does not decide whether replacement is given — and a
normal one does not excuse it. On every result.

## §3 The dose is per liter across the whole volume

Not per liter above the five-liter line. At 8 L that is **48–64 g**, not 18–24 g. The tile
computes the wrong answer alongside the right one and names it as the error the rule invites,
because the arithmetic reads both ways.

## §4 It converts to bottles, and says to check the strength

25% and 5% both carry 12.5 g per bottle; 20% carries 10 g. Concentrations differ between
formularies, so the tile gives the count and tells the reader to confirm what is stocked.

## §5 Two things it is not

- **Not nutritional support, and not a plasma expander of convenience.** The same product given
  for a low albumin alone does not have this evidence behind it.
- **Not the whole reason to tap.** Ascitic fluid should go for a cell count and culture whether or
  not the paracentesis is therapeutic, and this tile does not do that for you.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It computes a published dose from a volume already removed. It
does not decide whether to drain, and it does not prescribe.
