# spec-v887 — Occupational HIV exposure (PEP decision)

## What this gives you

Which branch of the US Public Health Service framework an exposure falls in — and it names no
drug, no dose and no regimen.

## §1 The framework

**An exposure that warrants evaluation**: a percutaneous injury; contact of a mucous membrane or
non-intact skin with blood or another potentially infectious material; a bite with blood
exposure. **Intact skin is not an exposure.**

| Source HIV status | |
|---|---|
| Known positive | Prophylaxis **recommended** |
| Unknown, or source unidentifiable | Not routinely recommended; **case by case**, on the likelihood the source is infected |
| Known negative | **Not recommended** |

## §2 The 2013 update removed the two-drug "basic" regimen

This is why the tile exists. Exposures were once tiered into basic and expanded regimens by
severity; that tiering is gone. Every recommended course is three drugs or more, and the severity
of the injury no longer selects between them. Printed on every result that is an exposure at all.

## §3 Do not wait for source testing

Prophylaxis should begin in **hours rather than days**, and can be stopped if the source is later
found negative. Waiting for a result is the delay that costs the most. When the tile is given
hours since the exposure it reads them back; beyond 72 hours it says that is a reason for expert
consultation, not for assuming nothing can be done.

## §4 Two readings that go the wrong way often enough to name

**Intact skin is not an exposure**, and **an unknown source is not a positive source**. On every
result.

## §5 It names no drug

No dose, no regimen. Those belong to the occupational health service or the treating clinician,
and expert consultation is advised for pregnancy, known or suspected resistance, and delayed
presentation. Because no antiretroviral is named, nothing in this tile can go stale as regimens
change.

Baseline and follow-up testing of the exposed person, and evaluation for hepatitis B and
hepatitis C — which follow separate rules — are named whenever the pathway is live.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- Kuhar DT, Henderson DK, Struble KA, et al. *Updated US Public Health Service guidelines for the
  management of occupational exposures to HIV and recommendations for postexposure prophylaxis.*
  Infect Control Hosp Epidemiol. 2013;34(9):875-892.

The citation names the US Public Health Service and CDC, so a `docs/citation-staleness.md` row is
owed and added.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies a published decision framework to an exposure already
described. It does not prescribe, and it does not replace occupational health review.
