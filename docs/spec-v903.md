# spec-v903 — Cuff leak test

## What this gives you

The leak volume and the leak fraction, read against **both** cutoffs in use — and the guideline's
answer to a question the number does not settle: who should have had this test at all.

## §1 The measurement

**Leak = inspired tidal volume (cuff up) − averaged expired volume (cuff down)**, averaged over
several breaths.

| Cutoff for a "small leak" | |
|---|---|
| Absolute | < 110 mL |
| Fraction | < 10–15% of the inspired volume |

The guideline does not fix one. The tile reports against both, says whether they agree at this
value, and offers neither as the answer — the posture of spec-v865, v881 and v886.

## §2 The test is recommended only in patients at high risk of post-extubation stridor

This is why the tile exists. Performed on everybody it delays extubations that did not need
delaying, because most patients who fail it never develop stridor. High risk is an **input**, and
when it is not recorded the tile says the test may not have been indicated. On every result.

## §3 A failed test is not an instruction to keep the tube in

For a patient who fails and is otherwise ready, the guideline suggests **systemic steroids at
least 4 hours before extubation** — and extubation need not be deferred beyond that. Printed
whenever either cutoff is failed.

## §4 The positive predictive value is poor

A small leak raises the probability of stridor without establishing it, and a normal leak does not
exclude it. On every result.

## §5 It refuses an impossible measurement

An expired volume larger than the inspired one cannot produce a leak, and the tile says the two
values are probably in the wrong fields rather than reporting a negative leak.

Secretions, tube size and patient effort all move the number, and a single discordant breath is
not a result.

  of Chest Physicians Clinical Practice Guideline: Liberation from Mechanical Ventilation in

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It computes a published comparison from two volumes already
measured. It does not decide whether to extubate.
