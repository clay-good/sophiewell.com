# spec-v898 — Surgery timing after coronary stenting

## What this gives you

Whether the elapsed interval clears the published minimum before elective noncardiac surgery —
and which of the three intervals applies.

## §1 The intervals

| After | Delay elective noncardiac surgery |
|---|---|
| Balloon angioplasty, no stent | at least **14 days** |
| Bare-metal stent | at least **30 days** |
| Drug-eluting stent | optimally at least **6 months**; after **3 months**, surgery may be considered when the risk of further delay outweighs the risk of stent thrombosis |

## §2 The stent type changes the answer by a factor of twelve

This is why the tile exists. Thirty days against a hundred and eighty — and which stent was placed
is not always in the note that mentions the stent. On every result.

## §3 The three-to-six month window is a judgment, not a green light

The tile names it as its own status rather than rounding it to "not yet" or "fine", and says it is
a shared decision with cardiology and surgery that nothing in the number settles.

## §4 The interval is about when to operate, not whether to stop the antiplatelet

Where the surgery permits it, aspirin is continued through the perioperative period, and stopping
both agents early is the exposure the interval exists to avoid. On every result — the two
questions are constantly conflated.

## §5 Urgent and emergency surgery is not delayed by any of this

An urgent operation is an input, and it changes the answer to "these intervals do not govern it"
rather than producing a shortfall the reader has to argue past.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It compares an elapsed interval against published minimums. It
does not schedule an operation, and it does not decide antiplatelet therapy.
