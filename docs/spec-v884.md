# spec-v884 — Eat, Sleep, Console

## What this gives you

What the approach does next, from three functional observations — and the fact that there is no
score here at all.

## §1 Three questions, asked of the infant as it is now

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eat** | Can it feed adequately, or is feeding limited by withdrawal? |
| **Sleep** | Does it sleep an hour or more after a feed? |
| **Console** | Can it be consoled within about ten minutes with caregiver support? |

Any one failing → **review and escalate non-pharmacologic care**, then reassess.
Still failing with that care maximized → **pharmacologic treatment is considered**, by the team.

## §2 It is not a score

This is why the tile exists. Nothing is added up; there is no total and no threshold. A reader
arriving from the Finnegan tile expects a number and there is not one, and the two are **not
interchangeable** — one medicates on a number, the other on function. On every result.

## §3 Non-pharmacologic care is the intervention, not a preliminary

Rooming-in, parental presence, holding, a quiet low-light room, feeding on cue and clustered care
are the treatment. The tile takes them as inputs, names the ones not recorded as in place, and
only says "medication is considered" once all of them are. Rooming-in gets called out by name
when it is missing.

## §4 An item counts only when the failure is attributable to withdrawal

A hungry infant who will not settle, or one unwell for another reason, is not an item failure —
and looking for that other reason is part of the assessment. "Another cause is suspected" is an
input, and it changes the answer.

## §6 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It records three functional observations against a published
approach. It does not decide whether to give medication.
