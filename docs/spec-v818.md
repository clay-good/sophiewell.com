# spec-v818 — ICHD-3 Criteria (Tension-Type Headache)

## What this gives you

Enter the pattern and the symptoms once; get whichever of 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3 the history meets.

Tension-type headache is the most common headache disorder there is, and it was the last
large gap in this catalog's headache family — after cluster headache, migraine, medication
overuse and trigeminal neuralgia.

## §1 The three subtypes

All three share **criterion C**: at least two of four — both-sided location; pressing or
tightening, non-pulsating; mild or moderate intensity; not aggravated by routine physical
activity.

| | Frequency | Episode duration |
|---|---|---|
| **2.1** infrequent episodic | ≥10 episodes, <1 day/month (<12 days/year) | 30 min – 7 days |
| **2.2** frequent episodic | ≥10 episodes, 1–14 days/month for >3 months | 30 min – 7 days |
| **2.3** chronic | ≥15 days/month for >3 months — **no episode count at all** | hours to days, or unremitting |

## §2 The rule that runs backwards

The associated-symptom criterion **loosens** in the chronic form, which is the opposite of
what a criteria set called "chronic" leads you to expect.

- **Episodic (2.1, 2.2)** — no nausea or vomiting *at all*, and no more than one of
  photophobia or phonophobia.
- **Chronic (2.3)** — no more than one of photophobia, phonophobia **or mild nausea**;
  excluding only *moderate or severe* nausea and vomiting.

So **mild nausea disqualifies the episodic forms and is permitted in the chronic one**, where
it spends the at-most-one allowance. Carrying the episodic rule across denies 2.3 to patients
who have it.

This is why nausea is a **severity select and not a checkbox**. "Nausea: yes/no" cannot
express the rule — it would either deny 2.3 to patients who qualify or grant 2.1 and 2.2 to
patients who do not. Tested in both directions, including that mild nausea *plus* a phobia is
two of the allowed three and therefore one too many.

## §3 Two smaller differences that also get flattened

**The chronic form has no episode-count requirement.** Only 2.1 and 2.2 ask for ≥10 episodes.
When the chronic frequency is met without one, the tile says so rather than appearing to
withhold a diagnosis.

**The duration wording is not the same interval.** The episodic forms say "30 minutes to 7
days"; the chronic form says "hours to days, or unremitting". A 45-minute headache satisfies
the first and not the second. The duration select therefore offers the published phrases
rather than one numeric range, and maps each to the subtypes it actually satisfies.

## §5 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published criteria to a history already taken.
It does not prescribe an abortive or a preventive.
