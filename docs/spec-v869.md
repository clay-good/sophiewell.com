# spec-v869 — EORTC/MSGERC invasive fungal disease definitions

## What this gives you

The consensus tier — proven, probable, or possible — with the thing about that framework that is
most often forgotten stated on the result itself: it is a research instrument.

## §1 The three tiers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Proven** | Fungal invasion shown by histopathology, cytopathology or direct microscopy from a normally sterile site, **or** a culture from a normally sterile site. **No host factor is required.** |
| **Probable** | A host factor **and** a clinical feature **and** mycological evidence. All three. |
| **Possible** | A host factor **and** a clinical feature, with no mycological evidence. |

Eight host factors, eight clinical features and five items of mycological evidence, as published.

## §2 They are research definitions

This is why the tile exists. They were written so that clinical trials of antifungal therapy
could be compared, not to decide whether an individual patient is treated. That prints on every
result.

## §3 "Possible" is an epidemiologic category, not a tier of treatment

It is the weakest rung in the framework, and a case that stops there is one in which mycological
evidence has not been obtained — not one in which disease has been established. Printed whenever
the case lands there.

## §4 Probable needs all three; proven needs none of them

Two of the three components is possible at best, and mycological evidence on its own classifies
nothing. In the other direction, proven stands on tissue invasion or a sterile-site culture
alone, and neither a host factor nor a clinical feature adds to it.

## §5 No host factor means these definitions do not apply

They assume an immunocompromised host. Without a host factor a case cannot reach probable or
possible however ill the patient is, and disease acquired in intensive care in a patient without
one is outside what the framework was built to describe. The tile says so rather than returning a
bare "not classified".

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published research definitions to findings already
recorded. It does not decide whether to start antifungal treatment.
