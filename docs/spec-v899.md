# spec-v899 — Periprocedural anticoagulant bridging

## What this gives you

Both periprocedural questions answered in order: whether to interrupt, and then whether to bridge.

## §1 The two branches

| | |
|---|---|
| **Direct oral anticoagulant** | Interrupted on the bleeding risk of the procedure and on renal function. **Never bridged** — the short half-life is the bridge |
| **Warfarin, low or moderate thrombotic risk** | Interrupted, **not** bridged |
| **Warfarin, high thrombotic risk** | Interrupted; bridging **considered**. Mechanical mitral valve, stroke or VTE within 3 months, or equivalent |
| **Minimal bleeding risk, any agent** | Interruption may not be needed at all |

## §2 The default is not to bridge

This is why the tile exists. Bridging was routine for years and the BRIDGE trial reversed it: in
most patients with atrial fibrillation it increases major bleeding without reducing
thromboembolism. The question is now whether this patient is one of the **few exceptions**, not
whether there is a reason to skip it. On every result.

## §3 A direct oral anticoagulant is never bridged

And its interruption is not timed on an INR — renal function and the bleeding risk of the
procedure set the interval, and impaired renal function lengthens it. On the warfarin branch the
tile still says this, because the reader may be about to meet the other case.

## §4 Ask about interruption before asking about bridging

Many procedures of minimal bleeding risk are done on anticoagulation. The tile puts that first
when the procedure is not minimal, so the bridging question does not get answered for a patient
who did not need to stop.

## §5 Resumption is its own decision

Timed on hemostasis rather than the calendar, and a therapeutic-dose resumption after a
high-bleeding-risk procedure is usually delayed further than a prophylactic one. Printed whenever
an interruption is in play.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published guidance to a drug, a procedure risk and a
thrombotic risk already characterized. It does not prescribe, and it does not set an interruption
schedule.
