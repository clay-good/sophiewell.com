# spec-v858 — WHO Grades of Hearing Loss

## What this gives you

The grade, from the ear the grade is actually defined on — with the asymmetric case separated
out instead of being forced onto the scale.

## §1 The grades

Each ear's average is taken over **0.5, 1, 2 and 4 kHz**. The grade is read from the **better**
ear.

| Grade | Better ear | |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | under 20 dB | No impairment |
| 1 | 20 to under 35 | Mild |
| 2 | 35 to under 50 | Moderate |
| 3 | 50 to under 65 | Moderately severe |
| 4 | 65 to under 80 | Severe |
| 5 | 80 to under 95 | Profound |
| 6 | 95 and above | Complete or total |

Even 15 dB steps, by design.

## §2 One deaf ear is its own category, not grade 5

This is the whole reason the tile exists.

A profoundly deaf ear beside a normal one has no honest place on the bilateral scale. Graded on
the **better** ear it is grade 0, which is what the scale literally says and which describes the
hearing but not the disability. Graded on the **worse** ear it is grade 5, which describes
neither.

The 2021 revision made it a separate category: better ear under 20 dB **and** worse ear 35 dB
or more is **unilateral hearing loss**. The tile returns that instead of a grade, and says why.

## §3 The grade is the better ear, and only the better ear

Stated on every result where the two ears differ. Reading the worse ear over-calls every
asymmetric loss on the scale.

## §4 Mild starts at 20 dB, not 26

The earlier WHO grading began at 26. Read against that line, everything from 20 to 25 dB is
normal — a whole 6 dB band of mild loss that disappears. The tile raises the point where the
better ear lands in it.

## §5 The average includes 4 kHz

A three-frequency average over 0.5, 1 and 2 kHz is a different number. In noise-induced and
age-related loss the high frequencies are the worst, so dropping 4 kHz lowers the average and
under-calls the grade. The tile takes all four and says which it used.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- World Health Organization. *World report on hearing.* Geneva: WHO; 2021.

WHO is a tracked issuer, so `docs/citation-staleness.md` carries a row.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies a published grading to thresholds already measured.
It does not interpret an audiogram's shape, distinguish conductive from sensorineural loss, or
select a device.
