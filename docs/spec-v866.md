# spec-v866 — Broset Violence Checklist

## What this gives you

The published total, with the two things a low score is most often taken to mean stated on the
result itself.

## §1 Six behaviors, one point each

Confused · Irritable · Boisterous · Physically threatening · Verbally threatening · Attacking
objects

| Total | |
|---|---|
| 0 | The risk of violence is small |
| 1 to 2 | Moderate. Preventive measures should be taken |
| 3 or more | Very high. Preventive measures required, and a plan for managing an attack |

## §2 It covers the next twenty-four hours, and nothing beyond them

This is why the tile exists. The checklist is scored on what is being observed **now** and
rescored every shift. A total carried over from a previous shift describes that shift, not this
one. The sentence prints on every result, including the zero.

## §3 Zero is small, not none

The published wording says the risk is *small*, deliberately. A total of 0 is not a clearance,
and the tile says so whenever the total is 0.

## §4 The score is an observation, not an intervention

It is never a justification for restraint or seclusion, which have their own legal and clinical
standards that this instrument does not address — the catalog covers the reassessment side of
that separately in `restraint-timer`. This prints on every result too.

## §5 Confusion is a behavior here, not a diagnosis

A disoriented patient scores the item whatever the cause, and the cause still has to be looked
for. Raised whenever that item is scored.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- Almvik R, Woods P, Rasmussen K. *The Broset Violence Checklist: sensitivity, specificity, and
  interrater reliability.* J Interpers Violence. 2000;15(12):1284-1296.

No tracked guideline issuer, so no `docs/citation-staleness.md` row is owed.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It records observed behavior on a published checklist. It does
not decide what to do, and it never authorizes restraint or seclusion.
