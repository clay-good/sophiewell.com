# spec-v853 — Echocardiographic Criteria for Constrictive Pericarditis

## What this gives you

Whether the echo meets the published criteria, and which reading of them — because the same
three findings give two different answers depending on how many you insist on.

## §1 The rule

Three findings, each independently associated with constriction:

| | |
|---|---|
| Respiration-related ventricular septal shift | required |
| Medial (septal) mitral annular e' | **≥ 9 cm/s** |
| Hepatic vein expiratory diastolic reversal ratio | **≥ 0.79** |

| Combination | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Septal shift **and** either of the other two | 87% | 91% |
| All three | 64% | 97% |

Septal shift is the anchor. Neither of the other two counts without it.

## §2 A normal medial e' is the abnormal finding — annulus reversus

This is the whole reason the tile exists.

In a heart with a stiff pericardium and a normal myocardium, longitudinal relaxation at the
**septal** annulus is preserved or exaggerated, while the lateral wall is tethered by the shell.
So the medial e' is **preserved or increased**, and it is frequently **higher than the lateral
e'** — the reverse of the normal relationship.

Read the usual way, a medial e' of 11 cm/s in a patient with heart failure looks reassuring and
gets used to argue against a filling problem. It is one of the two criteria. The tile says so
whenever the value lands at or above the line, and it names **annulus reversus** by its finding
whenever both annular velocities are entered and the medial one is the higher.

## §3 E/e' does not estimate filling pressure here — annulus paradoxus

The corollary. Because the medial e' rises with the severity of constriction, E/e' varies
**inversely** with left atrial pressure in this disease. The usual reading of E/e' is not just
imprecise here; it points the wrong way. Stated whenever the criteria are met.

## §4 The hepatic vein ratio is a division, so the tile does it

Expiratory diastolic reversal velocity ÷ diastolic forward velocity. The tile takes the ratio
directly, or the two velocities and computes it.

## §5 What it does not do

It does not distinguish constriction from restrictive cardiomyopathy on its own — that is the
comparison the criteria were derived against, and the numbers above are how well they separate
the two, not a verdict. It does not stage, and it does not select an operation.
