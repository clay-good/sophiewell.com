# spec-v838 — Clinical vs Preclinical Obesity (2025 Commission)

## What this gives you

Enter the measurements and the functional findings; get whether obesity status is confirmed
at all, and if so whether it is **clinical** or **preclinical**.

## §1 Two steps, and the first one is the reform

**Step 1 — confirm excess adiposity.** Any one of:

- direct body fat measurement (DXA or bioimpedance);
- **one** anthropometric criterion — waist circumference, waist-to-hip, waist-to-height —
  **in addition to** BMI;
- **two** anthropometric criteria, **regardless of** BMI.

One exception: above a **BMI of 40**, excess adiposity can pragmatically be assumed.

**Step 2 — classify.** Confirmed obesity is **clinical** where there are signs or symptoms of
reduced tissue or organ function due to the adiposity, or a substantial age-adjusted
limitation of day-to-day activities. Otherwise it is **preclinical**.

## §2 BMI alone is not a diagnosis

That is the entire reform. Below 40, a BMI on its own confirms nothing — the Commission
recommends it be used as a **population-level** surrogate for risk rather than to assess an
individual. A BMI of 38 with nothing else returns *not confirmed*, and the tile says why.

## §3 The miss runs both ways

Route three confirms obesity on **two anthropometric criteria regardless of BMI**. So a person
with a normal BMI, a raised waist and a raised waist-to-height ratio has **confirmed obesity**
— and a tool that screened on BMI first would never reach them. The tile says so explicitly at
exactly that combination.

## §4 Clinical versus preclinical is about function, not size

A higher BMI does not make obesity clinical. Nothing in step two reads a number: a BMI of 55
with preserved function is **preclinical**, and a BMI of 31 with organ dysfunction is
**clinical**. Tested both ways.

Organ dysfunction without confirmed adiposity is not a diagnosis either — step one is a
gate, not a hint.

## §5 The cutoffs are deliberately not encoded

The Commission specifies *"validated methods and cutoff points appropriate to age, gender, and
ethnicity"* rather than publishing one set. So the anthropometric inputs ask whether each
measure is **raised against an appropriate cutoff**, rather than taking raw centimetres and
applying thresholds the Commission declined to fix. The result says so whenever an
anthropometric criterion is used.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- Rubino F, Cummings DE, Eckel RH, et al. Definition and diagnostic criteria of clinical
  obesity. *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol.* 2025;13(3):221-262.
- The three confirmation routes, the BMI >40 exception and both category definitions were
  corroborated across two independent sources before encoding.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published criteria to findings already gathered.
It does not prescribe treatment or counsel on weight.
