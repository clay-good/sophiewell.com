# spec-v822 — 2017 Criteria (Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos)

## What this gives you

Enter the Beighton score, the twelve connective-tissue findings, the family history and the
exclusions; get whether all three of the 2017 criteria hold.

A companion gap: `beighton-hypermobility` has been here for a long time. The Beighton score
is criterion 1 of the hEDS criteria and not a diagnosis on its own, and the criteria it feeds
into were missing — which is precisely the gap that lets a Beighton score be read as a
diagnosis.

## §1 The three criteria

**1 — generalized joint hypermobility.** Beighton against an age- and sex-adjusted cutoff:
**≥6** pre-pubertal children and adolescents; **≥5** pubertal to age 50; **≥4** over 50.

**2 — two or more of three features.** A: five of twelve connective-tissue findings. B: a
first-degree relative independently meeting the criteria. C: at least one of three
musculoskeletal complications.

**3 — all three prerequisites.** No unusual skin fragility; other heritable and acquired
connective-tissue disorders excluded; alternative causes of hypermobility excluded.

All three must hold **simultaneously**.

## §2 The questionnaire rescue is exactly one point wide

If the Beighton score is **exactly one point below** the cutoff, two or more of the five-part
questionnaire items also satisfy criterion 1.

Two points below is not rescued — not by two positive items, and not by all five. The tile
tests that boundary in both directions, and when a score lands one point below it says
whether the questionnaire carried it and how many items were positive.

## §3 The rule that catches people, and where it bites

Inside criterion 3: in a patient who **has** an acquired connective-tissue disorder — lupus,
rheumatoid arthritis — an additional diagnosis of hEDS requires meeting **both feature A and
feature B**, and **feature C cannot be counted at all**.

So "any two of A, B and C" is wrong for exactly the patients most likely to be assessed for
this: those already carrying a rheumatologic diagnosis, in whom chronic pain and joint
instability are least specific. A tool applying the general rule there would diagnose hEDS on
A+C or B+C where the criteria forbid it.

The acquired-CTD question therefore sits with the exclusions rather than as a footnote, and
when feature C is present but disqualified the tile says so rather than silently discarding
it. Four assertions pin this: B+C fails with the disorder and passes without it, A+C fails,
and A+B passes.

## §4 One shape note

The age group is a **select**, not a number. The cutoff turns on pubertal status, not age
alone — an adolescent and a pubertal adult can be the same age and carry different cutoffs,
and no arithmetic on a birthday can decide between them.

## §5 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- Malfait F, Francomano C, Byers P, et al. The 2017 international classification of the
  Ehlers-Danlos syndromes. *Am J Med Genet C Semin Med Genet.* 2017;175(1):8-26.
- The Ehlers-Danlos Society's own diagnostic checklist, from which every threshold, the
  twelve feature-A items, the five questionnaire items and the acquired-CTD rule were taken
  verbatim.

## §6 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published criteria to findings already gathered.
It does not arrange genetic testing or physiotherapy.
