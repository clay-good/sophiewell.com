# spec-v906 — TACO vs TRALI

## What this gives you

Which way a post-transfusion respiratory event points — and the step that happens before the
question is answered at all.

## §1 Same door, different rooms

Both begin **within 6 hours** of a transfusion with **new hypoxemia and bilateral pulmonary
infiltrates**. What separates them is whether the picture is hydrostatic.

| Points to circulatory overload | Points to acute lung injury |
|---|---|
| Raised natriuretic peptide | No evidence of hydrostatic pulmonary edema |
| Positive fluid balance | No other risk factor for ARDS, or one present but stable |
| Raised central venous or pulmonary artery pressure | |
| Cardiogenic signs — S₃, widened pulse pressure, raised JVP | |
| Improvement with diuresis | |

## §2 The treatments diverge

This is why the tile exists. Circulatory overload is treated by **removing volume**; acute lung
injury is not — and a diuretic given to a patient who is not overloaded makes them worse. Getting
the direction wrong is harmful in a way that getting the label wrong is not. On every result.

## §3 Stop the transfusion and report the event for both

That step does not wait on the distinction, and nothing on this page is a reason to delay it. It
prints on every result, above the reasoning.

## §4 They can coexist

A patient can be volume overloaded **and** have acute lung injury at once. "Features on both
sides" is its own answer here, not a tie the tile breaks — and when it lands there the result says
to treat the picture in front of you rather than pick a label.

## §5 These are surveillance definitions

Written so cases can be counted and compared between centers, not applied at the bedside in the
first ten minutes. On every result.

When the entry criteria are not recorded the tile says which one is missing and that neither
definition is reached, rather than weighing features that have no case to attach to.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It weighs features already observed against published
definitions. It does not diagnose, and it does not decide whether to give a diuretic.
