# spec-v819 — ICHD-3 Criteria (Indomethacin-Responsive Headaches)

## What this gives you

One tile for the two headaches ICHD-3 defines partly by their response to a drug: **3.2
Paroxysmal hemicrania** and **3.4 Hemicrania continua**.

They are together because the clinical question is one question. Facing a strictly one-sided
headache with autonomic features, what you want to know is whether this is an
indomethacin-responsive headache — and answering only the paroxysmal half or only the
continuous half would be the same kind of half-answer as reporting migraine without aura
while ignoring migraine with aura.

## §1 The criteria

**3.2 Paroxysmal hemicrania** — ≥20 attacks; severe unilateral orbital, supraorbital and/or
temporal pain lasting **2–30 minutes**; ≥1 ipsilateral cranial autonomic sign **or**
restlessness; frequency **>5 per day**; **prevented absolutely by therapeutic doses of
indomethacin**; no better ICHD-3 explanation.

**3.4 Hemicrania continua** — unilateral headache present **>3 months** with exacerbations of
moderate or greater intensity; ≥1 ipsilateral autonomic sign, **or** restlessness **or
aggravation of the pain by movement**; **responds absolutely to therapeutic doses of
indomethacin**; no better ICHD-3 explanation.

## §2 A diagnostic criterion that is a drug

This is what makes these two unlike everything else in the classification, and it is the
reason the indomethacin question sits under its own heading rather than among the symptom
checkboxes.

**The indomethacin response is a criterion, not a consequence of the diagnosis.** Neither
diagnosis can be made until an adequate trial has been done *and has worked absolutely*. A
tool that treated the drug response as optional — or as something to consider after
diagnosing — would report a diagnosis the classification does not permit.

So when every other criterion is met and the trial is missing, the tile says exactly that,
by name. When the history does not otherwise fit, it stays quiet, because then the missing
trial is not the story. Both tested.

ICHD-3's own dosing note is carried: in an adult, oral indomethacin should start at ≥150 mg
daily and rise if needed to 225 mg daily.

## §3 The cluster-headache boundary

Paroxysmal hemicrania is the diagnosis most often mistaken for cluster headache. It shares
criterion C almost word for word and differs on the numbers:

| | Attack duration | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Cluster headache (3.1) | 15–180 min | 1 every other day – 8/day |
| Paroxysmal hemicrania (3.2) | **2–30 min** | **>5/day** |

Shorter, more frequent, and indomethacin-responsive. When the entered duration is too long
for 3.2 and the frequency is in cluster range, the tile points at 3.1 rather than returning a
bare "not met". Note also that **>5 per day is strictly greater than five** — exactly 5 fails.

## §4 One asymmetry worth not flattening

Criterion C is *not* identical between the two. Hemicrania continua accepts **aggravation of
the pain by movement** as an alternative to restlessness; paroxysmal hemicrania does not.
When that alternative is what carries C, the tile says which diagnosis it can and cannot
support. Tested in both directions.

## §6 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published criteria to a history already taken.
It does not start indomethacin or manage its gastrointestinal risk.
