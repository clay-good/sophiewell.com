# spec-v889 — Home oxygen qualifying criteria

## What this gives you

Whether a resting room-air measurement qualifies for long-term home oxygen — and the three
conditions on the measurement that decide it before the number does.

## §1 The thresholds

| | |
|---|---|
| **Qualifies outright** | PaO₂ ≤ 55 mmHg, or SpO₂ ≤ 88% |
| **Qualifies with a supporting finding** | PaO₂ 56–59 mmHg, or SpO₂ 89%, **plus** dependent edema suggesting congestive heart failure, pulmonary hypertension or cor pulmonale, or a hematocrit > 56% |
| **Does not qualify** | PaO₂ ≥ 60 mmHg, or SpO₂ ≥ 90% |

## §2 The measurement must be at rest, on room air, with the patient stable

This is why the tile exists. A value taken during an exacerbation, or on supplemental oxygen,
does not establish a chronic need — and a qualifying value obtained that way is the commonest
reason a prescription is later found not to have been supportable. Room air and clinical
stability are **inputs**, not assumptions, and the tile says which is missing.

## §3 89% qualifies only with a supporting finding

It sits one point from 88% and is routinely read as though it were on the same side of the line.
Printed whenever the result lands in the borderline band, and the borderline result names the
three findings that would carry it.

## §4 Coverage criteria and clinical evidence are not the same question

The 2020 ATS guideline recommends continuous oxygen for **severe** chronic resting room-air
hypoxemia in COPD and in interstitial lung disease, and does **not** recommend it for moderate
resting hypoxemia. A patient can meet a coverage rule the evidence does not support, and the
reverse. On every result.

## §5 These are resting criteria

Desaturation on exertion and nocturnal desaturation are assessed separately and are not what this
reads. On every result.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published criteria to a measurement already taken. It
does not prescribe oxygen, and it does not determine coverage.
