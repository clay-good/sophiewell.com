# spec-v888 — FeNO interpretation

## What this gives you

Which band a fractional exhaled nitric oxide measurement falls in for that patient's age — and
what the number is actually measuring.

## §1 Age-specific cutpoints

| | Low | Intermediate | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twelve years and older | < 25 ppb | 25–50 | > 50 |
| Under twelve years | < 20 ppb | 20–35 | > 35 |

## §2 It measures eosinophilic airway inflammation, not asthma

This is why the tile exists. A low value does **not** exclude asthma; it argues against
eosinophilic inflammation and against a response to an inhaled corticosteroid at that moment. On
every result.

## §3 The intermediate band is not "mildly raised"

The guideline's instruction for it is to interpret cautiously and in the clinical context — which
is a different thing from treating a mid-range number as a weak positive. Printed when the result
lands there.

## §4 The cutpoints differ by age, and the tile says what the other set would give

The same **40 ppb** is intermediate in an adult and high in a child under twelve. Every result
names the cutpoints it used and what the other age group's reading would have been, because that
is the mistake worth catching.

## §5 A great deal moves the number

Corticosteroids and active smoking lower it; atopy, allergen exposure and rhinitis raise it; a
recent spirometry or a nitrate-rich meal can move it. All four are inputs, and when a
number-lowering factor sits alongside a low result the tile says the low value argues less than
one without it. And a change over time in the same person says more than a single value against a
population cutpoint.

  interpretation of exhaled nitric oxide levels (FENO) for clinical applications.* Am J Respir
  Crit Care Med. 2011;184(5):602-615.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It reads a number against published cutpoints. It does not
diagnose asthma, and it does not decide whether to start or change a corticosteroid.
