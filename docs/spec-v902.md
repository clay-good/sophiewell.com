# spec-v902 — Medical adhesive-related skin injury

## What this gives you

Whether it is a MARSI at all, and which of the seven injuries — with the rule that decides the
first question.

## §1 The criterion, then the family

**MARSI is erythema or another skin abnormality persisting 30 minutes or more after the adhesive
is removed.** Anything that fades sooner is not one.

| Family | |
|---|---|
| **Mechanical** | Skin stripping · tension injury or blister · skin tear |
| **Dermatitis** | Irritant contact · allergic |
| **Other** | Maceration · folliculitis |

## §2 The thirty-minute rule is the diagnostic criterion

This is why the tile exists. Erythema under a dressing that fades within half an hour of removal
is not an injury, and recording it as one inflates the count and moves attention away from the
injuries that are real. Persistence is an **input**, and without it the tile answers "not a
MARSI" no matter what was seen. On every result.

## §3 Irritant and allergic are told apart by distribution and timing

An irritant reaction stays **inside** the adhesive footprint and appears quickly. An allergic one
extends **beyond** it, needs prior sensitization, appears later, and recurs faster on re-exposure.
The tile says this when either is selected — and says it records which was **seen**, not which was
proven.

## §4 A skin tear from adhesive removal is both things at once

It is a mechanical MARSI **and** a classifiable skin tear ([spec-v900](spec-v900.md)). Recording
only one of them loses either the mechanism or the wound, and the tile says to record both.

## §5 It is mostly a technique problem

Removal angle, skin preparation, adhesive selection and whether the skin was allowed to dry are
what change the rate. The category starts that question rather than ending it. On every result.

With [spec-v900](spec-v900.md) and [spec-v901](spec-v901.md), the catalog now separates the four
mechanisms of skin damage that get written up as one another: trauma, moisture, pressure, and
adhesive.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- McNichol L, Lund C, Rosen T, Gray M. *Medical adhesives and patient safety: state of the
  science. Consensus statements for the assessment, prevention, and treatment of adhesive-related
  skin injuries.* J Wound Ostomy Continence Nurs. 2013;40(4):365-380.

No tracked guideline issuer, so no `docs/citation-staleness.md` row is owed.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It records a finding against a published consensus. It does not
diagnose an allergy, and it does not choose an adhesive.
