# spec-v878 — KDIGO membranous nephropathy risk

## What this gives you

Which of the four KDIGO risk categories a case falls in, and why the proteinuria on its own is
not the answer.

## §1 The four categories

| | |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Preserved eGFR, proteinuria < 3.5 g/day, serum albumin > 3.0 g/dL — **or** proteinuria fallen by more than half after six months of supportive therapy |
| **Moderate** | Preserved eGFR, proteinuria > 3.5 g/day not halved over six months of supportive therapy, and no high-risk feature |
| **High** | eGFR < 60; **or** proteinuria > 8 g/day beyond six months; **or** preserved eGFR with proteinuria > 3.5 g/day **and** one of: albumin < 2.5 g/dL, anti-PLA2R > 50 RU/mL, raised urinary α₁-microglobulin or IgG |
| **Very high** | Life-threatening nephrotic syndrome, or a rapid unexplained fall in kidney function |

## §2 The category is what the guideline acts on

This is why the tile exists. The number a reader arrives with is the proteinuria, and the
guideline does not act on it alone — heavy proteinuria in someone who has not yet had six months
of supportive therapy is a *moderate*-risk picture, not an indication in itself. On every result.

## §3 Six months of supportive therapy is part of the definition

The low and moderate categories are separated by what happened over that period, so the answer
changes with **time**, not only with the numbers. Said explicitly on a moderate result where that
period has not been recorded.

## §4 A high anti-PLA2R does not raise the category on its own

It counts only alongside proteinuria above 3.5 g/day. Printed when a high titer is entered
without nephrotic-range proteinuria — the case where a reader would expect it to matter and it
does not.

## §5 Very high risk is a clinical picture, not a number

Nothing in the laboratory panel produces it. On every result.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It applies published risk categories to values already recorded.
It does not decide whether to start immunosuppression.
