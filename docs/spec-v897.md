# spec-v897 — Preoperative fasting intervals

## What this gives you

Whether the elapsed interval meets the minimum for what was last taken — and, when it overshoots,
that the overshoot is a harm rather than a margin.

## §1 The table

| Last intake | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Clear liquids | 2 hours |
| Breast milk | 4 hours |
| Infant formula | 6 hours |
| Nonhuman milk, or a light meal | 6 hours |
| Fried or fatty food, or meat | 8 hours or more |

## §2 These are minimums, not targets

This is why the tile exists. "Nothing by mouth after midnight" is the practice the guideline was
written against: prolonged fasting is **not** safer, and it causes thirst, hypoglycemia,
irritability and hypovolemia without reducing aspiration risk. On every result — and when the
interval runs four or more hours past the minimum, the tile says so directly and names clear
liquids up to two hours before induction as the alternative to continued abstinence.

## §3 The clock runs to induction, not to the scheduled time

A delayed case does not extend a fast that has already been served; it **lengthens** it, which is
the harm above. On every result.

## §4 "Clear" means clear

Water, pulp-free juice, carbonated drinks, black tea and black coffee qualify. Anything with milk
in it does not, and neither does alcohol. Printed when clear liquids are the intake selected —
the one where the definition is actually in dispute.

## §5 What is outside the table

Emergency surgery, impaired gastric emptying, and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists.
And the intervals never guarantee an empty stomach. On every result.

## §6 Sourcing (spec-v97 gate)

- American Society of Anesthesiologists Task Force on Preoperative Fasting. *2023 American
  Society of Anesthesiologists Practice Guidelines for Preoperative Fasting.* Anesthesiology.
  2023;138(2):132-151.

The ASA is not in the tracked-issuer pattern, so no `docs/citation-staleness.md` row is owed.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It reads a published table against an interval already elapsed.
It does not clear a patient for anesthesia, and it does not overrule the anesthesia team.
