# spec-v855 — ICSD-3 Narcolepsy Criteria, Type 1 and Type 2

## What this gives you

Whether the criteria are met and which type — with the two rules that are most often applied
backwards spelled out on the result.

## §1 The criteria

Both types need **daily irrepressible sleep or lapses into sleep for at least 3 months**.

**Type 1** needs one of:

| | |
|---|---|
| Cataplexy **and** a positive sleep-latency test | mean latency ≤ 8 min **and** ≥ 2 sleep-onset REM periods |
| CSF hypocretin-1 | ≤ 110 pg/mL, or below a third of the normal mean |

**Type 2** needs all of: a positive sleep-latency test, **no** cataplexy, hypocretin not measured
or above 110 pg/mL, and the picture not better explained by something else.

A sleep-onset REM period on the **preceding overnight study** may replace one of the two on the
latency test.

## §2 Cataplexy alone is not the diagnosis

This is the first reason the tile exists.

The old classification named the disease "narcolepsy with cataplexy", and the habit survived the
rename. Under these criteria cataplexy is **half** of one route: it has to be paired with the
latency-test findings, or replaced entirely by a low hypocretin.

So when cataplexy is recorded without a positive latency test and without a hypocretin, the tile
says the criteria are not met **and names what is missing** rather than returning a bare no.

## §3 The overnight study can supply one of the two REM periods

The second. A latency test with one sleep-onset REM period reads as negative and gets filed as
such, when a REM period on the overnight study the night before completes the pair. The tile
counts them together and says when the substitution is what made the test positive.

## §4 A low hypocretin is type 1 even with no cataplexy

The third. Hypocretin deficiency **is** the disease; cataplexy is a consequence that not every
patient has. A result at or below 110 pg/mL settles the type on its own.

## §5 The same latency test comes back positive on too little sleep

Insufficient sleep, untreated obstructive apnea, a delayed sleep phase, and medication or its
withdrawal all produce a short latency and sleep-onset REM periods. Type 2 carries an explicit
exclusion for exactly that, and the tile will not return type 2 until it is recorded.
