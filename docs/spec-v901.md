# spec-v901 — GLOBIAD (incontinence-associated dermatitis)

## What this gives you

The category — and, when the findings actually describe pressure damage, a tile that says so
instead of categorizing it anyway.

## §1 Four categories

| | No signs of infection | Signs of infection |
|---|---|---|
| **Persistent redness, no skin loss** | 1A | 1B |
| **Skin loss** | 2A | 2B |

## §2 It is not a pressure injury

This is why the tile exists. Moisture damage written up as a "stage 2" is the commonest miscoding
in skin assessment, and the mechanisms are **opposite**: this is **top-down** damage from moisture
and irritants over skin that has been wet; a pressure injury is **bottom-up** over a bony
prominence. The distinction changes the prevention plan and the incident report, not just the
word. On every result.

## §3 The tile asks the two questions that separate them, and pushes back

"Over a bony prominence" and "distinct edges" are inputs that **do not change the category**.
They are there so the tile can say when the findings point the other way:

- **Both** — damage over a prominence with distinct edges is the pattern of a pressure injury, and
  the tile says categorizing it here may be the wrong call and to reassess before recording.
- **One** — it names which feature points at pressure and restates the moisture pattern.
- **Neither** — it states the moisture pattern for comparison.

This is the same derive-then-check shape as spec-v900, applied to a distinction rather than a
type.

## §4 The two can coexist

The presence of one does not exclude the other, and a patient with both needs both addressed.
On every result.

## §5 The B subcategory is a prompt, not a diagnosis

It records that clinical signs of infection were **seen** — not that infection is diagnosed, and
not that an antimicrobial is indicated. On an A result the tile notes that candidiasis is easy to
miss early and is worth re-checking. The category does not choose a product either way.

## §7 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It records a finding against a published categorization. It does
not diagnose infection, and it does not stage a pressure injury.
