# spec-v835 — Acromegaly Biochemical Diagnosis (IGF-1 + OGTT)

## What this gives you

Enter the IGF-1, the OGTT growth hormone nadir and which assay the laboratory used; get
confirmatory, consistent, excluded, or discordant.

## §1 The tests

**IGF-1**, against an **age- and sex-matched** reference. Above **1.3×** the upper limit of
normal, with typical clinical features, is confirmatory.

**The 75 g OGTT**, reading the **GH nadir** within 2 hours. A nadir below the assay threshold
*with a normal IGF-1* excludes the diagnosis.

IGF-1 is entered as a **multiple of the upper limit**, not a raw concentration: assays and
reference ranges differ enormously, and the criterion is itself expressed as a multiple.

## §2 Three things that decide the answer and are routinely got wrong

**A random GH is not a diagnostic test.** Growth hormone is secreted episodically by normal
and adenomatous pituitaries alike, and the guideline recommends *against* single random
measurements. A raised random level supports nothing on its own — the only thing it can do is
contribute to **exclusion**, and only below 0.4 µg/L alongside a normal IGF-1. The field
exists precisely so the tile can say that; omitting it would let a reader supply the number
somewhere else and believe it counted.

**The nadir threshold depends on the assay** — below **1.0 µg/L** conventional, below **0.4**
ultrasensitive. A tool using one figure for both gets one of them wrong, in opposite
directions. A nadir of 0.7 is *suppressed* on a conventional assay and *not* on an
ultrasensitive one, and the tile says so in exactly that 0.4–1.0 window, staying quiet outside
it where the assays agree.

**Exclusion needs both halves.** A suppressed GH with a raised IGF-1 does not exclude
acromegaly, and a normal IGF-1 with an unsuppressed nadir does not establish it. Discordance
is reported as a result in its own right, calling for further evaluation rather than a
verdict.

## §3 An age-matched range or none at all

IGF-1 falls with age. A laboratory range that is not age- and sex-specific cannot answer this
question, so the tile asks whether the reference was matched and says so when it was not.

## §5 Posture

Decision support, not a verdict. It interprets results already obtained. It does not arrange
imaging or treatment.
